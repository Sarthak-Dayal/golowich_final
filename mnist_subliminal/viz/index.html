What the last layer is doing in subliminal MNIST

Every model is a 784 → H → H → 13 MLP. Outputs 0–9 are the digit logits, and outputs a1–a3 are three extra "aux" logits. A digit logit is just W3[d] · h(x): one row of the last layer dotted with the H penultimate features. The teacher is trained with cross-entropy on 0–9 only, so its aux rows never change. The student starts from the same initialization and is trained only to copy the teacher's aux logits on uniform-noise images, so its digit rows never change. Cross-model students do the same thing but start from a different model's initialization. 10 models per width, the original script's hyperparameters, MPS.

3. Which hidden units make digit 0's logit, and are they the same units in the student?

Contribution of hidden unit j to the digit's logit on images of that digit, relative to the average image: W3[d, j] · (mean h_j on digit d − mean h_j on all digits). Summed over all units this is exactly how far that logit sits above its average. Bars show the 16 units that help the teacher most. The student shares the teacher's initialization, so its unit j is the "same" unit, and the digit row W3[d] is identical in both. The cross-model student's unit j comes from a different initialization, so there's no correspondence.

6. Tuning distillation and varying the number of aux logits M

Separate grid, 6 models per cell. For every width H and aux-logit count M, the student was distilled with learning rates 1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3 and 3e-3, and scored after 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 epochs. The best learning rate and epoch count were chosen on test images 0–4999 and are reported on 5000–9999. The cross-model student is scored at that same setting. The paper's setting is M = 3, lr 3e-4, 5 epochs.
